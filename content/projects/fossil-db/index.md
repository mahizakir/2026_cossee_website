Fossil DB is a database and image-annotation tool for plant–insect interactions in Alberta's fossil record. Alberta's superb Late Cretaceous (73–66 Ma) and early-to-middle Paleocene (66–60 Ma) fossil floras have yielded thousands of leaf specimens carrying well-preserved traces of insect damage. The database lets researchers catalogue these specimens, annotate insect-damage traces, and study how plant–insect communities responded to the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event.

## Why this matters

The relationship between plants and insects spans nearly 400 million years — a perpetual tug-of-war that has shaped the evolutionary pathways of both groups and produced the diverse modern biota underpinning all terrestrial ecosystems. As ecosystems face rapid anthropogenic climate change, the fossil record of plant–insect interactions offers an irreplaceable archive for understanding how these biotas respond to upheaval. Alberta's record across the K–Pg boundary is one of the strongest windows we have into ecosystem recovery after global extinction.

## What the database supports

- Persistent storage of fossil specimen metadata and images
- User management to safeguard data integrity
- Cropping and annotation of leaf images from fossil photographs
- Data download and export
