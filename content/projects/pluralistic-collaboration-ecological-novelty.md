For decades, conservation biology and ecology have wrestled with how to understand and manage introduced or 'invasive' species and increasing ecological novelty. Consensus among invasion biologists claims that introduced species are a leading cause of biodiversity loss. This consensus has become the basis for national and global policies that spend millions of dollars a year controlling or eradicating introduced organisms.

However, many scientists have criticized the consensus of invasion biologists, arguing that much of our understanding of introduced organisms is incomplete and unsubstantiated by evidence. These controversies have led to acrimonious accusations of 'science denialism' and to unclear recommendations to policymakers.

We are engaging in pluralistic collaboration to explore controversial questions in conservation biology and ecology, while pioneering new methods of doing science. We represent a spectrum of values and empirical perspectives on introduced species and ecological novelty. By meeting in small groups and employing tools of pluralistic collaboration, we are mapping the dimensions of our disagreements and codesigning fair tests of the empirical ones.

Given the diversity of viewpoints, the current cohort of 18 researchers is only a starting point, one that provides the framework and tools to begin this process. The guiding documents are downloadable below and include a process manifesto and ethics.
