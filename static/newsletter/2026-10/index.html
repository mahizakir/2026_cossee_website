03In the field

Six things from outside the lab. Our own output is §05.

Synthesis & open science

1. metafor 5.2-1 changes some variance equations, so your old numbers may move

Viechtbauer, metafor v5.2-1, 15 September 2026 (previous release 5.0-1, 26 April). metafor-project.org

Two numerical corrections, not cosmetic ones: the equations for vtype="LS2" on measures ROMC, VRC and CVRC, and a bug in conv.2x2() where ci and di cells could be wrongly replaced under replace="all". Also new: hettest(), vtype="LS3" for SMD, and a warning when standardised mean differences look implausibly large.

metafor is the package most of our meta-analyses are built on. Scripts still run, but those three measures and some reconstructed 2×2 tables now return different values than in April. Record the version in your session info.

2. Meta-analysts don't practise what they preach: 15% share code, 0% assess risk of bias

preprintDimri, …, Lagisz, …, Sánchez-Tójar, "Meta-analysts must lead by example and embrace all FAIR principles", EcoEvoRxiv. full text · data and code 10.5281/zenodo.22706517

Across 81 meta-analyses published 2016–2020 in ecology, evolution and environmental science: data sharing 68%, code sharing 15%, reporting-guideline uptake 22%, and risk-of-bias assessments 0%. No association between FAIRness and either guideline use or citation rate, which the authors read as an absence of incentives.

The 0% is the empirical floor under the Culina et al. argument we ran last issue, and the 15% is what Lauren Cadwallader's SORTEE plenary is about to address. Losia Lagisz is a co-author, so not strictly outside the lab.

3. Nature retracts a 337-citation drought paper over a date-parsing bug

Retraction note for Gebrechorkos et al., Nature (4 June 2025); retraction published 2 September 2026. 10.1038/s41586-026-11027-z

Four errors: grid cells not area-weighted in global averages, the wrong GLEAM version, a timestepping error where dates were not parsed correctly when subsetting, and inconsistent application of the arid-regions mask. The authors self-reported and chose retraction over correction. Retraction Watch counts 337 Web of Science citations at retraction and quotes the lead author saying no conclusions are affected (their position, not an independent assessment).

A heavily cited environmental result undone by a coding error, well after it entered the citation stream. Worth re-checking retraction status at the update stage of a living review.

4. Taxonomic bias is real and pervasive, and smaller than usually claimed

Mammola, Fontaneto, Bellvert, Chialva & Adamo, "The biodiversity we ignore: a global meta-analysis on taxonomic bias", Ambio, 21 September 2026. 10.1007/s13280-026-02479-8

905 effect sizes from 78 studies over three decades. Bias toward vertebrates is widespread across study designs, but the authors report the magnitude as "generally small (Pearson's r < 0.5)". They also detect publication bias within this literature, with smaller studies reporting inflated effects.

A meta-analysis of a meta-research claim the field repeats as settled: it confirms the direction, deflates the size, and diagnoses small-study effects in the bias literature itself.

Ecology & evolution

5. Pollination specialisation has no simple latitudinal gradient

Sakhalkar et al., "Global patterns in plant–pollinator specialization", Nature Ecology & Evolution, 30 September 2026. 10.1038/s41559-026-03170-7

More than 3,400 networks and over 110,000 interactions. Specialisation is latitudinally structured but not monotonic: it peaks at low northern latitudes near the tropical–subtropical boundary rather than at the equator, and climate predicted it better than latitude, biodiversity or productivity.

A mega-compilation overturning a gradient that has been treated as a given, and therefore used as an unexamined moderator, in network meta-analyses.

6. Summit floras are warming-shifted, but warming barely explains it

Hausharter et al., "Widespread thermophilization but weak link to climate warming in Europe's summit plant communities", Nature Ecology & Evolution, 14 August 2026. 10.1038/s41559-026-03150-x

724 plots, 53 European summits, 21 years. Mean thermophilization 0.06 per decade (s.d. 0.20), positive in 63% of plots, but temperature change explained almost nothing at plot scale (mean R² = 0.02 for macroclimate, <0.01 for microclimate). The strongest single predictor was availability of thermophilic colonisers (partial R² = 0.34).

A consistent aggregate signal resting on near-zero explanatory power at the unit of analysis: the heterogeneity-versus-mean-effect problem in its clearest empirical form.
